## Project Overview

This project focused on the design, construction, testing, and performance analysis of a Pelton hydraulic turbine for small-scale sustainable electricity generation. The team studied turbine operation, designed components in SolidWorks, manufactured prototypes using 3D printing, performed simulations in SolidWorks and Ansys, and successfully generated electricity from a working prototype.

## Introduction

The objective of the project was to design and build a Pelton water turbine capable of generating electricity while improving understanding of fluid mechanics, turbine design, CAD modeling, simulation, and manufacturing processes.

---

## System Components

### Pelton Wheel
- Bucket-based wheel designed to extract kinetic energy from water jets.
- Inspired by industrial Pelton turbine geometry.
- Manufactured using 3D printing.

### Shaft
- Transfers rotational energy from the wheel to the generator.
- Designed for stable operation with minimal vibration.

### Generator
- Converts rotational energy into electrical energy.

### Support Structure
- Holds and aligns the turbine components.
- Controls water evacuation and system stability.

### Nozzle
- Accelerates and directs water toward the wheel buckets.
- Optimized using CFD simulations.

### Ball Bearings
- Custom-designed and 3D printed.
- One of the most challenging components of the project.

### Transmission System
- Initially explored gears.
- Final prototype used an adapter-based transmission approach.

### Biomimicry
- Bucket separator geometry inspired by natural structures such as shells and seed pods.
- Improved flow distribution and energy capture.

## Structural Analysis

### Turbine Bucket
- Applied force: 64.22 N
- Maximum stress: 26 N/mm²
- Maximum displacement: 2.8 mm

### Nozzle Support
- Applied force: 8.32 N
- Maximum stress: 1.96 N/mm²
- Maximum displacement: 0.584 mm

### Reinforced Support
- Tested with loads up to 100 N
- Maximum displacement reduced to approximately 0.102 mm
